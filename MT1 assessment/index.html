Introduction

The Taj Mahal is a magnificent white marble mausoleum located in Agra, India. It was commissioned by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan in memory of his beloved wife Mumtaz Mahal. The construction of the Taj Mahal began in 1632 and was completed in 1653, employing thousands of artisans and craftsmen.

Architectural Significance

The Taj Mahal is renowned for its stunning architectural beauty, which combines elements from Islamic, Persian, Ottoman Turkish, and Indian architectural styles. The central dome is flanked by four minarets, and the entire complex is set within formal gardens that enhance its aesthetic appeal.

Cultural Importance

The Taj Mahal is not only a symbol of love but also a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It attracts millions of visitors from around the world each year and is considered one of the New Seven Wonders of the World. Its cultural significance extends beyond India, representing a universal symbol of beauty and romance.

Symbolism & Symmetry

Every element of the Taj Mahal was designed around bilateral symmetry and Islamic symbolism. The four surrounding minarets stand 40 meters tall and tilt slightly outward so that, in the event of an earthquake, they would fall away from the central tomb rather than damaging it. In front of the mausoleum lies the traditional Charbagh (four-part garden), divided by reflecting water channels that symbolize the four flowing rivers of paradise.

Visitor Information

The Taj Mahal is situated on the southern bank of the Yamuna River in Agra, Uttar Pradesh. It is open to visitors daily from 30 minutes before sunrise until 30 minutes before sunset, except on Fridays when the complex is closed for afternoon prayers at the mosque. Visitors can also book special night-viewing tickets on full moon nights and the two nights before and after.
